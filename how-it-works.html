Guide

How Fieldnote works

Fieldnote is a trip organiser. You choose where you are going, build the days, and end up with a plan that shows the order of the day, the travel time between stops and what it costs in Australian dollars. This page walks through the whole thing, including what is free and what is not.

01

Start from a field guide or from nothing

Both routes end in the same place. Most people start from a guide because the hard numbers are already there.

Every destination field guide opens with a Trip Facts strip. It is the same five lines on every page, so you can compare two destinations without reading two thousand words:

  • Best season, written against the Southern Hemisphere calendar
  • Flight time from Sydney, direct where a direct service exists
  • Daily budget in AUD, given as a range for budget, mid-range and comfortable travel
  • Visa requirement for Australian passport holders, with a link to the official source
  • Time difference from AEST, so you know when you can call home

From a guide you can copy the three-day sample plan straight into the planner and edit it, or start with a blank set of days and build from scratch.

02

Build the days

The planner is a list, not a map. That is deliberate: maps are good at showing where things are and bad at showing whether a day is possible.

  1. Add a day

    A trip starts with two empty days. Add or remove days at any time. Days renumber themselves, so deleting day two does not leave you with a plan that reads one, three, four.

  2. Add time-stamped stops

    Each stop takes a time, a name and a cost in AUD. Stops appear in the order you add them. Put in the ferry, the walk, the lunch and the drive: the point is to see the day, not to curate it.

  3. Record travel time between stops

    This is the field people skip and then regret. Ninety minutes between two stops is the difference between a relaxed afternoon and a day that collapses. In our published itineraries every gap carries a travel time and a distance in kilometres.

  4. Watch the totals

    The summary panel updates as you type: number of days, number of stops, estimated trip cost, average cost per day, and the total once you multiply by the number of travellers. Nothing is hidden behind a click.

  5. Check the day against reality

    Two useful rules. First, no more than one thing before lunch that requires a car park. Second, if the travel times in a day add up to more than three hours, it is a transit day, so plan it as one.

03

Book, export and travel

Fieldnote does not take bookings and does not hold your money. You book directly with providers.

Booking

Where we link to an accommodation, flight, car hire or tour provider, that link is labelled at the point it appears. If you book through it we may earn a commission at no additional cost to you. You are free to ignore the link entirely and book anywhere. See the Affiliate Disclosure for the full explanation.

Exporting

Pro accounts export a trip to a formatted PDF, which is the version worth having on your phone when you land somewhere with no signal. Free accounts can print the plan from any browser: the site has a print stylesheet that strips navigation, images and advertising so you get a clean two-page itinerary.

Sharing

Pro accounts can share a read-only link with the people you are travelling with, which is more useful than sending six screenshots to a group chat. Nobody needs an account to open a shared link.

Offline

The PDF export is designed for offline use: no images, high contrast, addresses written out in full, and the day cost summary on the first page. Print it too if you are going somewhere your phone battery will not survive.

Good to know

Everything on this page works in a normal browser. There is no app to install, nothing to download automatically, and no pop-up window.

05

How we make money

Six revenue lines, stated plainly. None of them change what a guide says.

  • Pro subscriptionsOur main revenue. A monthly or annual fee for the paid features listed above.
  • Affiliate commissionsAccommodation, flight, car hire and tour partners may pay us a commission when you book through a link on our site. The price you pay is not increased. Every link is labelled.
  • Paid custom itinerariesA one-time fee for a trip plan that a human planner has written and revised with you.
  • Agent and white-label plansMonthly subscriptions for Australian travel agents and small tour operators who build client itineraries on our platform.
  • Travel insurance referralsAn optional add-on. We may be paid a referral fee. We are not a licensed financial adviser and do not recommend a policy.
  • Display advertisingLabelled ad slots on guide and blog pages. Never on legal pages, never above the main content of the page.
