Itinerary 02 · Tasmania · 7 days

Seven days in Tasmania

A southern-to-northern loop that never asks for more than three hours of driving in a day, finishing at Cradle Mountain with two nights to wait out the weather.

01

Overview

Fly into Hobart, out of Launceston or Devonport. That single decision saves roughly four hours of backtracking, and most airlines price an open-jaw domestic booking the same as a return. If your fare rules make that awkward, reverse day seven and drive back to Hobart instead.

Seven days is enough for the south, the east coast and Cradle Mountain if you accept that the west coast and Strahan do not fit. Adding Strahan turns this into a ten-day trip. Attempting it in seven means two days that are entirely driving.

Two nights at Cradle Mountain is the most important structural decision here. Dove Lake is frequently in cloud. With one night you get one attempt at the walk; with two you almost always get a usable morning.

Who this suits

  • Two adults sharing a car and a room, which is what all costs assume
  • Anyone with a standard week of annual leave plus two weekends, which makes it nine days away for five days of leave
  • Moderate walkers. The longest walk here is the Wineglass Bay lookout return, about 1 hour 30 minutes with a steep climb
02

Day 1

Day 01

Arrive Hobart, kunanyi and Salamanca

Estimated day cost$186

  • 10:30 Land at Hobart, collect the hire car Declare or bin any fresh fruit, vegetables or honey. Tasmanian quarantine applies to mainland arrivals and is enforced. Drive 20 min · 17 km to the city
  • 11:30 Salamanca Place and Battery Point Sandstone warehouses, then up the Kelly Steps into Battery Point and around Arthur Circus. Ninety minutes on foot. The Salamanca Market runs Saturdays only. Walk 10 min
  • 13:00 Lunch at Constitution Dock Fish punts moored at the dock are the cheap option, around $18. Sit-down places nearby run to $35. Drive 30 min · 21 km up the summit road
  • 15:00 kunanyi / Mount Wellington summit 1,271 metres, with a boardwalk to the lookout. Often ten degrees colder than the city with serious wind. The road closes in snow, so check the Hobart City Council road status before driving up. Drive 35 min · 24 km back down
  • 17:00 Check in, central Hobart Three nights in one place removes a lot of packing and unpacking. Walk 25 min or drive 8 min
  • 18:30 Dinner in North Hobart Elizabeth Street has the density. Book Friday and Saturday.

Assumptions: hire car and fuel split between two, one lunch, one restaurant dinner, mid-range Hobart accommodation. Driving 1 h 25 min across 62 km.

03

Day 2

Day 02

Bruny Island

Estimated day cost$214

  • 07:45 Drive to Kettering ferry terminal Leave early in summer. The vehicle ferry queue can add 40 minutes and sailings are booked online. Drive 40 min · 35 km, then ferry 20 min
  • 09:15 The Neck lookout A 279-step boardwalk to a platform over the isthmus joining North and South Bruny. Twenty minutes, and the best orientation on the island. Little penguins come ashore here after dark in season. Drive 25 min · 22 km south
  • 10:30 Cape Bruny Lighthouse The second-oldest lighthouse in Australia, in service from 1838. The guided tower tour is worth the ticket. Very exposed, so take a windproof layer in any season. Drive 30 min · 26 km north
  • 12:30 Lunch: cheese, oysters and berries The island producers cluster within a few kilometres of each other around Great Bay. Allow $45 a head if you graze across three or four of them, which is the point. Drive 20 min · 15 km to Adventure Bay
  • 14:30 Adventure Bay beach walk Flat, sheltered and good for a slow hour. Water sits around 15°C in February, so swimming is for the committed. Drive 25 min, ferry 20 min, drive 40 min
  • 18:00 Back in Hobart Book the return sailing in advance during January.

Includes the return vehicle ferry, food bought from island producers and a lighthouse tour. A guided day tour from Hobart costs more but removes the ferry logistics. Driving 1 h 55 min plus 40 min of ferry.

04

Day 3

Day 03

Port Arthur and the Tasman Peninsula

Estimated day cost$198

  • 08:00 Leave Hobart for the peninsula Fuel up at Sorell on the way through. Drive 55 min · 62 km to Eaglehawk Neck
  • 09:00 Tessellated Pavement, Tasmans Arch and the Blowhole Three short coastal stops within ten minutes of each other. The Pavement is best at low tide. Drive 25 min · 22 km
  • 10:30 Port Arthur Historic Site A UNESCO World Heritage convict site. Entry includes a guided walking tour and a harbour cruise, and it is valid for two consecutive days. Allow three hours minimum. It is also a place where 35 people were murdered in 1996, and the memorial garden is treated accordingly. Lunch on site
  • 14:30 Remarkable Cave and Palmers Lookout Ten minutes from the historic site and usually empty. Stairs down to a viewing platform inside the sea cave. Drive 1 h 30 min · 100 km to Hobart
  • 17:30 Back in Hobart Last night in the city. Pack for an early start.

Port Arthur entry is the largest single cost of the day and is cheaper booked online. Driving 2 h 50 min across 184 km.

05

Day 4

Day 04

Hobart to Coles Bay and Freycinet

Estimated day cost$236

  • 08:30 Check out and drive north The Tasman Highway follows the coast. Buy a Holiday Parks Pass now if you have not: it covers every national park for eight weeks. Drive 1 h 15 min · 90 km to Orford
  • 10:00 Spiky Bridge and Kelvedon Beach A convict-built bridge made of vertical fieldstones, ten minutes south of Swansea. Fifteen minutes, free, and nobody stops there. Drive 15 min · 12 km
  • 11:00 Swansea and Great Oyster Bay Coffee with the Hazards across the water. There is an oyster farm on the highway just north of town. Drive 45 min · 47 km to Coles Bay
  • 13:00 Lunch in Coles Bay, then check in Limited options. The pub and one or two cafes, around $28 a head. Drive 10 min · 6 km to the park gate
  • 15:00 Wineglass Bay lookout A steep 1.3 kilometre climb on stone steps to the saddle, about 1 hour 30 minutes return. Late afternoon has better light and far fewer people than mid-morning. Carry water. Drive 10 min back to Coles Bay
  • 17:30 Cape Tourville Lighthouse boardwalk A flat 600 metre loop with the widest view on the peninsula, and it takes twenty minutes. Wheelchair accessible.
  • 19:00 Dinner in Coles Bay Book. There are not many tables in town.

Includes the parks pass amortised across the week. If the weather is poor, swap the lookout walk to tomorrow morning. Driving 2 h 35 min across 155 km.

06

Day 5

Day 05

East coast to Launceston

Estimated day cost$208

  • 08:00 Honeymoon Bay before breakfast Five minutes inside the park gate, orange lichen on granite, and empty at eight in the morning. Drive 50 min · 45 km north to Bicheno
  • 09:30 Bicheno Blowhole and the foreshore walk Ten minutes at the blowhole, longer if the swell is running. The coastal path continues past it. Drive 45 min · 55 km north
  • 11:00 Bay of Fires, Binalong Bay White sand, blue water, granite boulders covered in orange lichen. The southern end at Binalong Bay is the accessible part. Allow ninety minutes to walk and swim if the weather allows. Drive 30 min · 25 km to St Helens
  • 13:00 Lunch in St Helens The largest town on the east coast, so the widest choice since Hobart. Drive 1 h 15 min · 90 km inland
  • 15:00 St Columba Falls A 20-minute return walk through rainforest to a 90 metre fall. A short detour off the Tasman Highway and almost always quiet. Drive 1 h 15 min · 95 km to Launceston
  • 17:30 Check in, Launceston If you arrive with light left, Cataract Gorge is a fifteen-minute walk from the centre.

A driving day with short stops rather than long walks. Driving 3 h 55 min across 310 km, which makes it the longest total on the trip. Cut St Columba Falls if you are tired.

07

Day 6

Day 06

Launceston, Tamar Valley and on to Cradle Mountain

Estimated day cost$252

  • 08:30 Cataract Gorge A river gorge in the middle of a city. Walk the Cataract Walk on the northern side, about 40 minutes return. The chairlift across the basin is the longest single-span chairlift in the world and takes six minutes. Drive 25 min · 20 km north
  • 10:30 Tamar Valley cellar door Cool-climate sparkling and pinot noir. Most charge a tasting fee that is waived on purchase. Nominate a driver: the limit is 0.05 and enforcement is active. Drive 40 min · 40 km west
  • 12:30 Lunch in Deloraine A Georgian town on the Meander River, halfway to the mountain. Around $26 a head. Drive 1 h 20 min · 85 km to Cradle Mountain
  • 15:00 Check in near the park boundary Accommodation sits outside the park at the visitor centre end. Fuel and food are limited, so buy anything you need in Deloraine or Sheffield. Shuttle bus 20 min from the visitor centre
  • 16:30 Enchanted Walk and Pencil Pine Falls A flat 20-minute loop from the visitor centre through button grass and myrtle. A gentle first look while you see what the weather is doing.
  • 19:00 Dinner at the lodge There is no alternative within 40 minutes, so eat where you are staying.

Includes the chairlift, a tasting fee and the higher cost of accommodation at Cradle. Private vehicles are restricted beyond the visitor centre in peak periods, so the shuttle is compulsory. Driving 3 h 05 min across 200 km.

08

Day 7

Day 07

Dove Lake, then Launceston or Devonport

Estimated day cost$246

  • 07:30 First shuttle to Dove Lake The earliest service gives you the best chance of still water and cloud off the summit. Book the park pass and shuttle the night before. Shuttle 20 min from the visitor centre
  • 08:00 Dove Lake Circuit A 6 kilometre loop, largely boardwalked, around two to three hours. It passes the Ballroom Forest and the boatshed. Carry a waterproof layer, a warm layer, water and food: this walk turns from mild to serious in under an hour and does so regularly, even in February. Shuttle 20 min back
  • 11:30 Lunch at the visitor centre, then check out If cloud has closed the lake, the alternatives are the Rainforest Walk or the Waldheim Chalet loop, both short and both sheltered. Drive 1 h 25 min · 90 km to Sheffield
  • 14:00 Sheffield, town of murals Thirty minutes, and a reasonable last stop before the airport or ferry. Drive 35 min · 33 km to Devonport, or 1 h 15 min to Launceston
  • 16:00 Return the car and depart Refuel before the airport. If you are taking the Spirit of Tasmania, check-in for evening sailings closes well before departure.

Includes the park pass share, shuttle and final night of accommodation carried into the day rate. Driving 2 h 40 min across 180 km if finishing in Launceston.

What this excludes

  • Flights to and from Tasmania
  • Travel insurance
  • Optional extras such as a scenic flight, a Gordon River cruise or a guided walk
  • Any excess reduction taken at the car hire counter

Ways to spend less

  • Travel in late March or early November rather than January. Accommodation is the single largest line and it moves the most.
  • Buy the Holiday Parks Pass rather than paying per park. It pays for itself at the second park.
  • Self-cater breakfast and one other meal a day. Hobart and Launceston have full supermarkets; the small towns do not.
  • Do Bruny Island by ferry with your own car rather than on a guided tour.
10

Before you go

  • Book the hire car when you book flights. The Tasmanian fleet is genuinely limited and sells out over Christmas and Easter.
  • Do not drive between dusk and dawn if you can avoid it. Wildlife strike is the most common way visitors damage a car here, and most rental agreements exclude it.
  • Pack a waterproof shell and a warm layer in every month of the year. This is the thing mainland visitors get wrong most often.
  • Quarantine. No fresh fruit, vegetables, plants or honey into Tasmania, including from the mainland.
  • Mobile coverage drops out in national parks and along the west coast. Download offline maps before leaving a town.
  • Log your walk. Use the registration books at the start of longer walks.
  • Check park alerts with the Tasmania Parks and Wildlife Service and fire warnings with the Tasmania Fire Service before travelling into remote areas.
  • Emergency number in Australia is 000.
