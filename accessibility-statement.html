Accessibility Statement

Fieldnote Travel is built to the Web Content Accessibility Guidelines 2.1 at Level AA. This page describes what that means in practice, what we know is not yet right, and how to tell us when we have got something wrong.

Template notice

This statement is a starting template prepared for a website build. The conformance claim and known limitations must be verified by an accessibility audit of the live site, and the document reviewed by an Australian-qualified lawyer, before launch.

1. Our commitment

Travel planning should not require good eyesight, a steady hand or a mouse. We aim to meet WCAG 2.1 Level AA across every page of this site, including the trip planner and the printed and exported versions of an itinerary.

We currently describe the site as partially conformant, which means most of the standard is met and the exceptions are listed in section 6. We would rather say that plainly than claim full conformance we have not independently verified.

2. What we have built in

  • Semantic structure. Every page uses real HTML landmarks: a banner header, a navigation region, a main region, complementary sidebars and a footer. Headings run in order without skipped levels.
  • Skip to content. The first focusable element on every page is a skip link that jumps past the navigation.
  • Text contrast. Body text meets a minimum contrast ratio of 4.5 to 1 against its background, and large text meets 3 to 1. Muted text, placeholder text, error text and footer text were all checked individually rather than assumed.
  • Descriptive alt text. Every photograph carries alt text describing what is in it. Decorative images are not used.
  • No information conveyed by colour alone. The active navigation item is marked in the page markup as well as underlined; form errors carry text, not just a red border.
  • Reflow. The layout works from 320 pixels wide upwards without horizontal scrolling, and content reflows rather than being cut off at 400 per cent zoom.
  • Target size. Interactive controls are at least 44 by 44 pixels with spacing between them.
  • Reserved space for images and ad slots, so the page does not shift under your cursor as it loads.
  • Text resizing. Zoom is not disabled and the viewport does not set a maximum scale.

3. Keyboard operation

Everything on this site can be operated with a keyboard alone.

  • Focus is always visible, with a high-contrast outline that is never removed.
  • Tab order follows the visual order of the page.
  • The mobile menu is a dialog that traps focus while open, returns focus to the menu button when closed, and closes on Escape.
  • Accordions are real buttons with expanded state exposed to assistive technology, and the panel is associated with its trigger.
  • Filter controls are toggle buttons with pressed state exposed, and the result count is announced through a live region.
  • Form errors are associated with their field and announced when they appear.
  • There are no keyboard traps, no custom cursor behaviour and no right-click blocking.

4. Motion and animation

Motion on this site is restrained by design: a short fade and rise as sections enter the viewport, a slight image scale on card hover, and a hairline shadow on the header when you scroll.

All of it is disabled when your operating system or browser reports a preference for reduced motion. Nothing autoplays, nothing loops indefinitely, nothing moves without you scrolling, and there is no parallax, no video and no carousel that advances by itself.

Content is never hidden behind an animation. If scripting fails or is blocked, every section is visible.

5. Content and language

  • The page language is declared as Australian English.
  • Link text describes its destination. There are no bare "click here" links.
  • Data tables use proper header cells with scope, and captions describing what the table shows.
  • Long tables scroll horizontally within their own container, so the page itself never scrolls sideways.
  • Abbreviations and jargon are expanded on first use.
  • Prices, times and distances are written in a consistent format: Australian dollars, 24-hour time, kilometres, degrees Celsius, and dates as DD/MM/YYYY.

7. How we test

  • Keyboard only. Every page is walked through with the mouse unplugged before it ships.
  • Screen readers. Tested with the screen reader built into the platform on Windows, macOS, iOS and Android.
  • Zoom. Checked at 200 and 400 per cent, and at 320 pixels wide.
  • Contrast. Every foreground and background pair in the design system is calculated, not eyeballed.
  • Reduced motion. Every page is loaded with the preference enabled.
  • Automated checks run against each page, understanding that automated tools catch perhaps a third of real issues.
  • Independent audit. We intend to commission a formal audit by an external accessibility specialist, and will publish the result and the remediation plan on this page.

8. Reporting a barrier

If something on this site does not work for you, please tell us. You do not need to know why it is broken or what the standard says.

  • Email access@fieldnotetravel.com.au
  • Call +61 2 8000 0000, 9:00 to 17:00 AEST, Monday to Friday
  • Write to Accessibility, Fieldnote Travel Pty Ltd, Suite 4, Level 2, 118 Kippax Street, Surry Hills NSW 2010
  • Use the contact form and select Accessibility

Tell us the page, what you were trying to do, and what happened. If you can, mention the browser and any assistive technology you use, but do not worry if you cannot.

Our commitment: we acknowledge within 5 business days with a plan and a timeframe. Where a fix will take time, we will offer a workaround or provide the information another way in the meantime.

9. Alternative formats

If a page is not usable for you, we will supply the same information in another format at no charge. That includes plain text, large print, or reading a guide to you over the phone. Email access@fieldnotetravel.com.au and tell us what would work.

Every itinerary can also be printed using the site's print stylesheet, which produces a high-contrast, image-free, single-column document that works well with screen readers and with magnification.

10. Legal context

The Disability Discrimination Act 1992 (Cth) makes it unlawful to discriminate against a person on the basis of disability in the provision of goods, services and facilities, which includes online services. The Australian Human Rights Commission has published guidance recommending WCAG 2.1 Level AA as the appropriate standard for Australian websites.

We treat accessibility as an obligation rather than a feature. If you believe we have discriminated against you, you may complain to us using the details above, and you may also complain to the Australian Human Rights Commission. Complaining to us first does not limit that right.
