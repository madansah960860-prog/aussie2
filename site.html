01

A planner built around how Australians actually travel

Long flights, limited annual leave, school holidays that differ by state, and a dollar that moves. Fieldnote is written and priced for departures from Sydney, Melbourne, Brisbane, Perth and Adelaide, not translated from somewhere else.

Annual leave goes further

Every itinerary shows total days away, working days used and which public holidays you can lean on. A ten-day trip that only costs six days of leave is a different decision.

Costs in AUD, not converted later

Daily budget ranges, transport, entry fees and food estimates are all recorded in Australian dollars so the total on screen is the total you plan against.

Southern Hemisphere seasons

Best-time-to-visit advice is written from an Australian calendar. When our December is peak summer and Japan is deep winter, the guide says so plainly.

02

Three steps from idea to a plan you can print

No account is needed to try the planner. Nothing is charged until you choose to upgrade.

  1. Choose a destination and a rough window

    Start from one of our destination field guides or type your own. Each guide opens with a Trip Facts strip: best season, flight time from Sydney, a realistic daily budget in AUD, the visa position for Australian passport holders and the time difference from AEST.

  2. Build the days

    Add days, add time-stamped stops, record travel time between them and put a cost against each one. The planner totals the day, the trip and the per-person figure as you type. Drag nothing, learn nothing: it is a list.

  3. Book, export and travel

    Follow our links to compare accommodation, flights, car hire and tours, or book wherever you like. Export the plan to PDF for offline use on Pro, or print it straight from the browser on any plan.

04

What a Fieldnote day-plan card looks like

Time-stamped stops, travel time between them, and an estimated cost for the day in AUD. This is a real day from our three-day Great Ocean Road itinerary.

Day 01

Melbourne to Lorne

Estimated day cost$214

  • 07:30 Collect hire car, Southbank Book the smallest car that fits your luggage. Parking in coastal towns is tight in summer. Drive 1 h 20 min · 95 km to Torquay
  • 09:00 Torquay and the Memorial Arch Coffee, a walk at Point Danger, then the official start of the Great Ocean Road at Eastern View. Drive 35 min · 30 km to Aireys Inlet
  • 11:00 Split Point Lighthouse, Aireys Inlet Short cliff-top loop, about 40 minutes. Windy most days, take a layer. Drive 25 min · 20 km to Lorne
  • 13:00 Lunch on the Lorne foreshore Allow around $30 a head for a casual sit-down meal. Drive 15 min · 10 km to Erskine Falls
  • 15:00 Erskine Falls and the Erskine River walk Around 240 steps down to the base. Give yourself an hour and a half.
  • 18:00 Check in, Lorne Two nights in Lorne beats one night in three different towns.

Day cost assumes two adults sharing: hire car and fuel share, one cafe breakfast, one casual lunch, one pub dinner, and mid-range accommodation.

Why the cards look like this

Most itineraries online read like a list of attractions. They leave out the two things that break a day: how long it takes to get between stops, and what the day costs once you add up parking, entry, coffee and dinner.

Every Fieldnote day card carries both. Travel time is shown in the gap between stops, in minutes and kilometres, so you can see when a day is too full before you are standing in a car park at four o'clock deciding what to cut.

  • Time-stamped stops in the order you will actually do them
  • Travel time and distance between each stop
  • An estimated daily cost in AUD with the assumptions written underneath
  • Notes on what to skip when the day runs late

06

How we make money

In plain English, because you should not have to guess.

  • Subscriptions Most of our revenue comes from people paying for Pro. That is the part we want to grow, because it does not depend on you booking anything.
  • Affiliate commissions If you follow a link from our site to an accommodation, flight, car hire or tour provider and book, we may earn a commission. It costs you nothing extra. Every one of those links is labelled where it appears.
  • Paid custom itineraries A one-time fee for a plan a human has reviewed and revised with you.
  • Travel agent and white-label plans Australian travel agents and small tour operators pay a monthly fee to build client itineraries under their own branding.
  • Travel insurance partner referrals An optional add-on. We are not an insurer or a licensed adviser and we do not recommend specific policies. If you use a partner link, we may be paid a referral fee.
  • Display advertising Guide and blog pages carry clearly labelled advertising. Advertisers have no influence over what we write, and Pro accounts see guides without display ads.

What commercial arrangements never do: change the order destinations appear in, change what a guide says about a place, or add urgency that is not real. We do not publish countdown timers or room-scarcity messages. Read the full affiliate disclosure.
