Affiliate Disclosure

Fieldnote Travel earns commission on some bookings made through links on this site. This page explains exactly how that works, what it never changes, and how to avoid it entirely if you prefer.

Template notice

This disclosure is a starting template prepared for a website build. It must be verified against the actual commercial arrangements in place and reviewed by an Australian-qualified lawyer before the site goes live.

1. The short version

Some links on this site take you to accommodation, flight, car hire, tour or travel insurance providers. If you follow one of those links and make a booking, Fieldnote Travel Pty Ltd may be paid a commission by that provider or by an affiliate network.

  • It costs you nothing extra. The price you pay is the same as it would be if you went to the provider directly.
  • It does not change what we recommend. We are paid the same regardless of which option within a category you choose, and many of the places we mention have no affiliate programme at all.
  • Every affiliate link is labelled where it appears, not only on this page.
  • You can ignore them. Nothing on this site requires you to book through us.

2. How affiliate links work

An affiliate link contains a code that identifies Fieldnote as the source of the click. When you click it, the provider or an affiliate network records that the visit came from us, usually by setting a cookie in your browser. If you then make a booking within a defined period, the provider pays us a percentage of the booking value or a fixed fee.

Commission rates vary by category and by provider, typically between 2 and 8 per cent of the booking value for accommodation, and a fixed fee for some other products. We do not disclose individual commercial rates because they are set by the provider under a confidentiality term, but we do disclose the arrangement itself, which is the part that matters to you.

The commission is paid by the provider out of its own margin. It is not added to your price, and providers with rate parity obligations are contractually prevented from charging more through an affiliate channel than direct.

4. How affiliate links are labelled

Australian Consumer Law requires that commercial relationships be disclosed clearly, and the Australian Competition and Consumer Commission has been explicit that a disclosure buried on a separate page is not sufficient. We agree with that, so:

  • A disclosure block appears on the same page and near the link, in the sidebar of destination guides and directly above or below the relevant section.
  • The disclosure is in plain English, in the same size type as body text, and is not hidden behind an expander.
  • The disclosure states that we may earn a commission and that the price to you does not change.
  • Affiliate links carry the appropriate link relationship attributes in the page markup.

5. What a commercial arrangement never changes

These are commitments, not aspirations, and you should hold us to them.

  1. The order of destinations. The destination index is ordered editorially. No provider can pay to move a destination up it.
  2. What a guide says about a place. If a place is not worth the detour, the guide says so. If a season is bad, the guide says so.
  3. Where-to-stay recommendations. No property has paid to be listed in a where-to-stay section, and we list areas rather than individual properties precisely so that the recommendation cannot be bought.
  4. Cost estimates. Budget tables are built from the bottom up and are not adjusted to make a partner look better.
  5. Editorial independence. No advertiser or affiliate partner sees a guide before publication, and none can request a change to one.
  6. No manufactured urgency. We do not display countdown timers, room-scarcity messages, or claims that a price will rise. Those tactics increase affiliate revenue and we do not use them.

Our largest revenue line is Pro subscriptions, not commission, and that is deliberate. A business that depends on you booking something has an incentive to push you towards booking something.

6. Travel insurance referrals

Travel insurance is a regulated financial product in Australia. Fieldnote Travel Pty Ltd is not an insurer, not an insurance broker, and does not hold an Australian Financial Services Licence.

Where we refer you to a licensed insurance partner, we may be paid a referral fee, and the referral link will say so at the point it appears. A referral is not a recommendation. We do not compare policies, rank insurers, or advise on whether a policy suits you.

Before acquiring any policy, read the Product Disclosure Statement and Target Market Determination, and if you need advice, speak to a licensed adviser. Our Travel Insurance Guide is general information only.

7. Display advertising

Guide and blog pages carry display advertising, which is a separate arrangement from affiliate commission. Advertising:

  • is clearly labelled as advertising and visually distinct from editorial content
  • never appears above the main content of a page
  • never appears on legal pages or the error page
  • is placed in a reserved space so it does not cause the page to shift as it loads
  • is not shown to logged-in Pro subscribers

Advertisers have no influence over editorial content and do not see it before publication.

8. What data is shared when you click

When you follow an affiliate link you leave our website. We do not send the partner your name, your email address or your account details. The affiliate network records a click identifier so a commission can be attributed; that identifier is not your identity.

The partner site sets its own cookies and collects its own information under its own privacy policy, which we do not control. Our handling of your personal information is described in the Privacy Policy, and the cookie we set to record an affiliate click is listed in the Cookie Policy. That cookie is only set if you consent to advertising cookies.

9. If you would rather not use them

Entirely reasonable, and easy:

  • Note the name of the place or provider, open a new tab, and go there directly.
  • Reject advertising cookies in the consent banner, which prevents the attribution cookie being set.
  • Subscribe to Pro, which removes display advertising from guide pages and supports the site directly.

None of these reduces your access to any content. Every guide, itinerary, checklist and tool works identically.

10. Why we disclose this way

Australian Consumer Law prohibits misleading or deceptive conduct in trade or commerce. The ACCC has published guidance making clear that where a business has a commercial interest in a recommendation, that interest must be disclosed clearly and prominently, close to the recommendation, in a way a consumer will actually see.

A single line in a footer, or a disclosure page nobody visits, does not meet that standard. Nor, in our view, does a grey four-point disclaimer at the bottom of a long article. That is why the disclosure block appears in the sidebar of every destination guide, beside every relevant section, and on this page in full.

If you think a link on this site is not disclosed clearly enough, tell us at hello@fieldnotetravel.com.au and we will fix it.
