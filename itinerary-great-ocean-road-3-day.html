Itinerary 01 · Victoria · 3 days

Three days on the Great Ocean Road

Melbourne to Port Campbell driving west, back inland on day three, with the Twelve Apostles at sunrise rather than in the middle of a coach queue.

01

Overview

This is the version of the Great Ocean Road that works. It drives west from Torquay, which puts you on the ocean side of the road so every lookout is a left turn. It splits the 243 kilometre touring route across two driving days rather than one, and it puts you in Port Campbell overnight so you can be standing at the Twelve Apostles at first light with a dozen people rather than three hundred.

The return on day three runs inland on the Princes Highway, which is faster and much duller. Do not be tempted to drive the coast road back: it adds roughly two hours and you will have seen all of it.

Who this suits

  • Two adults sharing a car and a room, which is what the costs assume
  • Anyone with a long weekend from Melbourne, or a Friday to Sunday added to a Melbourne work trip
  • Interstate visitors flying into Melbourne, since day one starts at 07:30 from the city
  • Families with older children. Several stops involve long staircases, so it is harder with a pram

What to change if you have four days

Add a night in Apollo Bay between days one and two. That lets you walk a section of the Great Ocean Walk, spend a proper morning in the Otways and take the Cape Otway Lightstation without rushing.

02

Day 1

Day 01

Melbourne to Lorne

Estimated day cost$214

  • 07:30 Collect the hire car, Southbank City depots open at 07:30 and cost less than the airport counters. Book the smallest car that fits your luggage: coastal town parking is tight. Drive 1 h 20 min · 95 km to Torquay
  • 09:00 Torquay, Point Danger and Bells Beach Coffee on Gilbert Street, then the lookout at Point Danger. Bells Beach is a seven-minute detour and worth it if there is swell. Drive 35 min · 30 km to Aireys Inlet
  • 10:15 Memorial Arch at Eastern View The official start of the road and the memorial to the returned servicemen who built it. Ten minutes, and there is a sculpture of the workers beside the arch. Drive 10 min · 8 km
  • 11:00 Split Point Lighthouse, Aireys Inlet A 40-minute cliff-top loop past the lighthouse, down to the beach and back. Almost always windy: take a layer. Drive 25 min · 20 km to Lorne
  • 13:00 Lunch on the Lorne foreshore Around $30 a head sitting down, or half that from the bakery on Mountjoy Parade. Drive 15 min · 10 km inland
  • 15:00 Erskine Falls Around 240 steps down to the base of a 30 metre fall through tree ferns. Ninety minutes including the climb back. Safe to cut if the day has run late. Drive 15 min · 10 km back to town
  • 17:00 Teddy's Lookout A five-minute walk from the car park to the best free view on the eastern half: the road curving across the St George River mouth. Drive 8 min back into Lorne
  • 18:00 Check in and dinner in Lorne Book dinner on a Friday or Saturday. Lorne fills quickly in summer.

Assumptions: hire car and fuel split between two, one cafe breakfast, one casual lunch, one pub dinner, mid-range accommodation in Lorne. Driving total 2 h 55 min across 173 km.

03

Day 2

Day 02

Otways, Apollo Bay and the Apostles

Estimated day cost$243

  • 08:30 Leave Lorne heading west The 45 kilometres to Apollo Bay is the most scenic driving of the trip. Pull over at Cape Patton and at Wye River. Drive 1 h · 45 km with two stops
  • 09:45 Coffee in Apollo Bay Last reliable supermarket before Port Campbell. Buy lunch supplies here. Drive 20 min · 17 km inland
  • 10:30 Maits Rest rainforest walk A flat 800 metre boardwalk loop through myrtle beech and tree ferns. Thirty minutes, wheelchair accessible for most of its length, and a complete change of scenery from the coast. Drive 25 min · 20 km to Cape Otway
  • 11:30 Cape Otway Lightstation Mainland Australia's oldest surviving lighthouse, operating since 1848. Paid entry, about 90 minutes. Koalas are often in the manna gums along Lighthouse Road: watch for cars stopped in the lane. Drive 1 h 10 min · 75 km to Princetown
  • 13:45 Lunch at Princetown Picnic tables by the Gellibrand River estuary, which is why you bought supplies in Apollo Bay. Drive 10 min · 8 km
  • 14:30 Gibson Steps 86 steps cut into the cliff face, putting you on the beach directly beneath the stacks. Closed at high tide and in heavy swell. Read the sign at the top before you go down. Drive 5 min · 3 km
  • 15:30 Twelve Apostles viewing platforms Busiest between 11:00 and 15:00, so this is the tail of the crowd. Forty-five minutes. You will come back tomorrow at dawn anyway. Drive 8 min · 5 km
  • 16:30 Loch Ard Gorge Four short walks from one car park, including the beach in the gorge and the cemetery above it. Half the visitors of the Apostles and, for our money, the better site. Drive 12 min · 12 km to Port Campbell
  • 18:00 Check in at Port Campbell Small town, three or four dinner options, all of which stop serving early. Book ahead.

Assumptions: lighthouse entry, picnic lunch, mid-range accommodation in Port Campbell and a restaurant dinner. Driving total 3 h 20 min across 185 km. Cape Otway is the stop to cut if you are behind.

04

Day 3

Day 03

Sunrise, the Shipwreck Coast, back to Melbourne

Estimated day cost$168

  • 06:15 Sunrise at the Twelve Apostles Ten minutes from Port Campbell and the single reason to have stayed there. Check the sunrise time for your date and arrive 25 minutes before it. Cold and windy in any season. Drive 12 min · 12 km back to town
  • 08:00 Breakfast in Port Campbell, then check out Around $22 a head. Drive 10 min · 8 km west
  • 09:15 The Arch and London Arch Two signposted stops within six kilometres. London Arch was a double span until part of it collapsed in 1990, leaving two visitors stranded on the outer section until a helicopter reached them. Drive 8 min · 6 km
  • 10:00 The Grotto A sinkhole and archway with a boardwalk and stairs down to a rock pool. Twenty minutes and usually quiet. Drive 25 min · 24 km
  • 10:45 Bay of Martyrs and Bay of Islands The quiet western end of the limestone coast. Two car parks, three lookouts, almost nobody. This is where the scenic route ends. Drive 35 min · 40 km to Warrnambool
  • 12:30 Lunch in Warrnambool From June to September, add an hour for Logans Beach, a shore-based southern right whale nursery with a viewing platform. Drive 3 h 15 min · 260 km inland via the Princes Highway
  • 17:00 Return the car in Melbourne Refuel at Colac or Geelong, not at the depot.

Cheapest day of the three because everything on the Shipwreck Coast is free to visit and free to park at. Driving total 4 h 15 min across 350 km.

What this excludes

  • Flights to Melbourne from other states
  • Travel insurance
  • Optional extras such as a helicopter flight over the Apostles or a surf lesson at Torquay
  • Any excess reduction taken at the car hire counter

Ways to spend less

  • Travel mid-week outside school holidays. Accommodation in Lorne can be close to half the weekend rate.
  • Skip Cape Otway Lightstation entry and walk the Maits Rest boardwalk instead. That is the largest single entry fee on the trip.
  • Buy lunch supplies in Apollo Bay both days rather than eating out.
  • Stay in Warrnambool instead of Port Campbell on night two. It is cheaper, but you lose the sunrise.
06

Before you go

  • Book the car early. Melbourne hire fleets tighten over Victorian school holidays and long weekends.
  • Download offline maps. Mobile coverage drops out through the Otways and in the gorges.
  • Check VicTraffic for road closures. The coast road closes occasionally after storms and landslips.
  • Fuel discipline. Fill in Torquay, then Apollo Bay, then Port Campbell.
  • Cliff edges are fenced for a reason. The limestone is actively eroding and sections fail without warning.
  • Swimming. Most beaches on this coast are unpatrolled with strong rips. Never swim at Gibson Steps or beneath the Apostles.
  • Dusk driving. Kangaroos on the Otway roads at dawn and dusk. Slow down or plan to be off the road.
  • Emergency number in Australia is 000.
