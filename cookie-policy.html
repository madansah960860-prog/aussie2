1. What a cookie is

A cookie is a small text file that a website asks your browser to store on your device. Cookies let a site remember things between page loads and between visits, such as whether you are logged in or what you chose in a consent banner.

Related technologies include local storage, which stores data in your browser without sending it to a server with each request, and pixels or tracking tags, which are small requests used to record that something happened. We treat all of these the same way as cookies for consent purposes.

2. How consent works on this site

No non-essential cookie is set before you make a choice. When you first arrive, a banner appears at the bottom of the screen with three options:

  • Accept all, which enables essential, analytics and advertising cookies
  • Reject non-essential, which leaves only essential cookies in place
  • Manage preferences, which lets you turn analytics and advertising on or off individually

Until you choose, only essential cookies are used. Rejecting non-essential cookies does not restrict your access to any content or feature on this site: every guide, itinerary and tool works the same either way.

The banner does not block the page, does not appear as a modal overlay on load, and does not prevent you using the browser back button.

6. Local storage used by the tools

Some tools on the site store data in your browser rather than on our servers. This is functional, not tracking:

  • The trip planner, used without an account, keeps what you type in the page only. It is not written to our servers and is lost when you close the tab.
  • The packing checklists keep tick state in the page only, and reset on reload.
  • Your cookie choice is stored in local storage so the banner does not reappear on every page.

7. Changing your choice

You can change your cookie preferences at any time. Clear the site's storage in your browser, or clear cookies for this site, and the consent banner will reappear on your next visit so you can choose again.

Withdrawing consent stops further non-essential cookies being set by this site. It does not delete data already collected by a third party; use the third party's own opt-out for that.

8. Browser controls

All major browsers let you block or delete cookies, and most offer a setting to block third-party cookies by default. Blocking all cookies, including essential ones, will prevent you logging in and will break parts of the planner.

Look under Settings, then Privacy, in Chrome, Firefox, Safari or Edge. Browser help pages explain the exact steps and change more often than we could keep up with.

We also honour the browser preference for reduced motion, though that is an accessibility setting rather than a cookie one. See the Accessibility Statement.
