Great Ocean Road

243 kilometres of coast from Torquay to Allansford, built by returned servicemen and best driven over three unhurried days.

01

Overview

The Great Ocean Road is a memorial. It was built between 1919 and 1932 by around 3,000 returned servicemen, largely by hand, and dedicated to those who died in the First World War. That is worth knowing before you drive it, because it explains why the road hugs the cliff rather than taking the sensible inland route.

The touring route runs 243 kilometres from Torquay in the east to Allansford near Warrnambool in the west. It divides neatly into three parts. The surf coast from Torquay to Lorne is cliff-edge driving with beach towns every twenty minutes. The Otways from Lorne to Apollo Bay turn inland into cool temperate rainforest, waterfalls and tall trees. The Shipwreck Coast from Princetown to Peterborough is the limestone section: the Twelve Apostles, Loch Ard Gorge, London Arch.

The single most common mistake is trying to do it as a day trip from Melbourne. It is technically possible, and it is nine hours of driving to spend forty minutes at a lookout in the middle of a coach tour. Three days is the honest minimum for the full route. Two days works if you skip the Otways.

Direction matters. Driving west from Torquay puts you on the ocean side of the road, which makes pulling into lookouts a left turn rather than a cross-traffic manoeuvre. Almost everyone drives west. If you want the Twelve Apostles without a crowd, drive east from Warrnambool instead and arrive at the Apostles at 08:00 rather than 14:00.

02

Best time to visit

Southern Victoria, so summer is December to February and winter is June to August. The coast is cooler and windier than Melbourne all year.

Season by season on the coast
SeasonMonthsTypical rangeNotes
SummerDec to Feb14°C to 24°CWarmest water, longest days, and the heaviest traffic of the year. Accommodation in Lorne and Apollo Bay books out and prices rise sharply over the January school holidays.
AutumnMar to May11°C to 21°CThe best window. March holds summer warmth without summer crowds, and low autumn light is kind to the limestone stacks.
WinterJun to Aug8°C to 14°CWild and dramatic, with whale watching off Warrnambool from June to September. Expect rain, short days and some walking tracks closed after storms.
SpringSep to Nov9°C to 18°CWaterfalls in the Otways run properly after winter rain. Changeable, but quiet mid-week.

Timing your day, not just your month

Coach tours from Melbourne arrive at the Twelve Apostles between 11:00 and 15:00. If you are staying in Port Campbell you can be at the viewing platform at sunrise with perhaps a dozen other people. Sunset is also good but busier. This one decision improves the trip more than any other on this page.

School holiday pressure

Victorian school holidays drive most of the demand, but New South Wales and South Australian holidays overlap in late June, late September and December. If your dates are flexible, the first three weeks of March and the last two weeks of October are consistently the best combination of weather, price and quiet.

What is free and what is not

Most of the headline sights cost nothing. The Twelve Apostles, Loch Ard Gorge, Gibson Steps, the Memorial Arch, Erskine Falls, Teddy's Lookout and every beach on the route are free, and parking at them is free. The paid options are the extras: a helicopter flight over the Apostles, the Otway Fly treetop walk, Cape Otway Lightstation entry, a surf lesson at Torquay, or a wildlife cruise from Apollo Bay.

  • Hire car. The main fixed cost. Melbourne Airport hire desks are convenient and dearer than city depots. Check the excess reduction price before you accept it at the counter.
  • Fuel. Fill up in Geelong or Torquay. Prices along the coast run higher and the stations between Apollo Bay and Port Campbell are sparse.
  • Accommodation in Lorne and Apollo Bay is the second largest cost and the one that moves most with season. Mid-week in autumn can be half the January weekend rate.

Three-day sample plan

Melbourne to Port Campbell, driving west, then returning inland. Costs are per person with two people sharing.

Day 01

Melbourne to Lorne

Estimated day cost$214

  • 07:30 Collect the hire car in Melbourne City depots open earlier than you expect and cost less than the airport. Drive 1 h 20 min · 95 km to Torquay
  • 09:00 Torquay and Point Danger Coffee, a look at Bells Beach if the swell is up, then east to the official start of the road. Drive 35 min · 30 km to Aireys Inlet
  • 11:00 Split Point Lighthouse, Aireys Inlet A 40-minute cliff-top loop past the lighthouse and back along the beach. Almost always windy. Drive 25 min · 20 km to Lorne
  • 13:00 Lunch on the Lorne foreshore Allow about $30 a head for a casual sit-down meal. Drive 15 min · 10 km inland to Erskine Falls
  • 15:00 Erskine Falls Around 240 steps to the base of a 30 metre fall. Ninety minutes including the walk back up. Drive 15 min back to town
  • 17:30 Teddy's Lookout, then check in The best free view of the road curving along the St George River mouth. Two nights in Lorne beats one night in three towns.

Assumes hire car and fuel shared between two, one cafe breakfast, one casual lunch, one pub dinner and mid-range accommodation in Lorne.

Day 02

Otways and Apollo Bay to Port Campbell

Estimated day cost$243

  • 08:30 Leave Lorne for Apollo Bay The most scenic 45 kilometres of the whole route. Pull over at Cape Patton and Wye River. Drive 1 h · 45 km with stops
  • 10:00 Maits Rest rainforest walk A flat 800 metre boardwalk loop through myrtle beech and tree ferns. Thirty minutes, and a complete change from the coast. Drive 25 min · 20 km to Cape Otway
  • 11:15 Cape Otway Lightstation Mainland Australia's oldest surviving lighthouse, in service since 1848. Entry is paid. Koalas are often visible in the manna gums along Lighthouse Road. Drive 1 h 10 min · 75 km to Princetown
  • 14:00 Gibson Steps 86 steps cut into the cliff, putting you on the beach beneath the stacks. Check the tide and the closure signs, they are not decorative. Drive 5 min · 3 km
  • 15:30 Twelve Apostles and Loch Ard Gorge Two hours covers both comfortably. Loch Ard Gorge has four short walks and half the people. Drive 15 min · 12 km to Port Campbell
  • 18:30 Check in at Port Campbell Small town, limited dinner options, book ahead in summer.

Includes lighthouse entry and a longer driving day. If you are short on time, Cape Otway is the stop to cut.

Day 03

Shipwreck Coast, then back to Melbourne

Estimated day cost$168

  • 06:30 Sunrise at the Twelve Apostles Ten minutes from Port Campbell and the reason to stay there. Expect a dozen people rather than three hundred. Drive 15 min back for breakfast
  • 09:00 The Arch, London Arch and The Grotto Three signposted stops within 12 kilometres west of Port Campbell. Twenty minutes each. Drive 25 min · 24 km to Bay of Islands
  • 10:45 Bay of Martyrs and Bay of Islands The quiet end of the limestone coast, and the official finish of the scenic route at Allansford. Drive 35 min · 40 km to Warrnambool
  • 12:30 Lunch in Warrnambool From June to September, Logans Beach is a shore-based southern right whale nursery worth an extra hour. Drive 3 h 15 min · 260 km inland via the Princes Highway
  • 17:00 Back in Melbourne The inland return is faster and duller. Do not attempt to drive the coast road back.

Lowest-cost day of the three because almost everything on the Shipwreck Coast is free to visit.

05

Getting around

Driving

You need a car. The road is sealed and two lanes the whole way, but it is genuinely winding between Lorne and Apollo Bay, with tight corners, blind crests and cyclists. Average speeds are lower than the posted limit suggests: budget 90 minutes for the 45 kilometres from Lorne to Apollo Bay if you stop at lookouts, which you will.

Drive on the left. This is worth stating plainly because a meaningful share of crashes on this road involve overseas visitors turning onto the wrong side after leaving a car park. Signs remind you at every major lookout. If someone is close behind you, use the marked slow-vehicle turnouts rather than speeding up.

Without a car

  • V/Line runs a train to Geelong and connecting coaches along the coast to Apollo Bay. It is slow but it works, and it is the cheapest way to reach Lorne or Apollo Bay for a few days of walking.
  • Day tours from Melbourne exist in large numbers. They cover the Apostles and little else, and involve about nine hours in a seat.
  • Small-group multi-day tours are a reasonable option if nobody in the party wants to drive.

Where to stay by area

Torquay

Only worth a night if you are surfing or arriving late from Melbourne. Plenty of motels and a supermarket, which the smaller towns lack.

Lorne

The best base for the eastern half. Walkable, a proper main street, restaurants that stay open, and Erskine Falls and Teddy's Lookout within fifteen minutes. It is also the most expensive town on the road in January.

Apollo Bay

More relaxed and slightly cheaper than Lorne, and better placed for the Otways. A good middle stop if you are doing four days rather than three.

Port Campbell

Small, plain and strategically perfect. Staying here is the only realistic way to reach the Twelve Apostles at sunrise. Around a dozen places to stay and three or four places to eat, so book both.

Warrnambool

A regional city at the western end with the widest choice of accommodation and the lowest prices on the route. Sensible if you are driving the road east to west and want to start early from the far end.

Safety notes

  • The road itself. Winding, narrow in places, with sheer drops and limited overtaking. Fatigue is the main risk. Swap drivers, and do not attempt Melbourne to the Apostles and back in one day.
  • Cliff edges. The limestone is actively eroding. Fencing and closures exist because sections collapse without warning. Stepping past a barrier for a photograph is how people die on this coast.
  • Beaches and rips. Most beaches on this coast are unpatrolled. Where a beach is patrolled, swim between the red and yellow flags. Never swim at the base of the Apostles or at Gibson Steps.
  • Tides at Gibson Steps. The beach access closes at high tide and in heavy swell. Check the sign at the top before you descend.
  • Wildlife. Kangaroos and koalas near the Otways at dawn and dusk. Reduce speed, and do not stop in a traffic lane to photograph them.
  • Weather. Cold fronts arrive quickly from the Southern Ocean. A calm morning can be a 60 km/h onshore wind by two in the afternoon.
  • Mobile coverage. Patchy through the Otways and in gorges. Download offline maps before you leave Torquay.

08

Good to know

  • Traditional owners. The route passes through Wadawurrung, Gadubanud and Eastern Maar Country. Several interpretive sites along the coast are developed with Traditional Owner groups.
  • Fuel. Fill in Torquay, Apollo Bay or Port Campbell. Do not run low through the Otways.
  • Food logistics. Buy lunch supplies before you leave a town. Between Apollo Bay and Port Campbell there is very little.
  • Parking. Free at all the major sights. The Twelve Apostles car park fills between 11:00 and 15:00.
  • Drones. Restricted or banned at Port Campbell National Park and other sections. Check Parks Victoria before you fly one.
  • Accessibility. The Twelve Apostles main viewing platform, Loch Ard Gorge upper lookouts and Maits Rest boardwalk have sealed or boardwalk access. Gibson Steps and Erskine Falls do not.
  • Power and connectivity. Australian Type I plug, 230 V. Your normal mobile plan applies; coverage is the only issue.
  • There are eight Apostles. Not twelve. There were nine when the name was applied, and one collapsed in 2005. Nobody minds.
