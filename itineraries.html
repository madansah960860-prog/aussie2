How to read a Fieldnote itinerary

Four things appear on every day card, and each of them exists because leaving it out is how plans fail.

  1. Time-stamped stops

    Every stop has a start time, in 24-hour format. The times are realistic rather than aspirational: they assume you eat breakfast, find parking and occasionally stand in a queue.

  2. Travel time and distance between stops

    Shown in the gap after each stop, in minutes and kilometres. These are door-to-door estimates with traffic, not the optimistic figure a mapping app gives at three in the morning.

  3. An estimated cost for the day

    In Australian dollars, per person, assuming two people sharing accommodation and transport. The assumptions are written at the bottom of each card so you can adjust them for your own trip.

  4. What to cut when the day runs late

    Each itinerary marks the stops that are safe to drop. Weather, illness and a place you liked more than expected are normal, not exceptions.

On the prices

Every figure in these itineraries is an estimate based on typical mid-range travel, not a quoted price. Costs move with season, exchange rate and availability. Check current prices with the provider before you commit to anything.
