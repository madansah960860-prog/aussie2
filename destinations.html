Destination field guides

Fourteen destinations Australians travel to most, each with the same five facts at the top so you can compare them honestly: best season on a Southern Hemisphere calendar, flight time from Sydney, a realistic daily budget in AUD, the visa position for Australian passport holders, and the time difference from AEST.

Cool-climate walking, produce and short distances. Hobart, Bruny Island, the east coast and Cradle Mountain sit inside a comfortable week.

North to south covers three separate climates, which is why a ten-day dash usually disappoints. Pick two regions. Australian passport holders need a visa or e-visa: check the Vietnamese immigration authority before booking. Mid-range budget around $120 a day.

Australia3 to 6 days

Red Centre

Uluru, Kata Tjuta and Kings Canyon. Go May to September: October to March regularly exceeds 38°C and walks close by mid-morning. Accommodation is limited and priced accordingly, so book months out. Budget $300 or more a day.

Flight time from Sydney
3 h 30 min to Ayers Rock (AYQ)
Time difference from AEST
-30 minutes

Australia4 to 7 days

Whitsundays

Sailing, snorkelling and Whitehaven Beach. May to October is dry, mild and outside stinger season, which is the main reason to avoid November to May in the water without a suit. Budget around $320 a day with a boat day included.

Flight time from Sydney
2 h 20 min to Proserpine (PPP)
Time difference from AEST
None, no daylight saving in Queensland

Australia3 to 5 days

Margaret River

Three hours south of Perth by road. Cellar doors, karri forest, caves and a serious stretch of coast. September to November is the sweet spot: wildflowers, fewer crowds and mild driving weather. Around $260 a day mid-range.

Escarpment country, rock art and waterfalls. The dry season, May to October, is the only sensible window for road access; the build-up from October is genuinely unpleasant. Crocodile safety is not optional here. Budget about $240 a day.

4 h 30 min to Darwin

November to February is dry and cooler in the north; the Andaman and Gulf coasts have opposite wet seasons, so the islands you pick determine the month you go. Australian passport holders currently receive visa-free entry for tourism, but always confirm the current rule. Around $130 a day mid-range.

Flight time from Sydney
9 h 20 min direct to Bangkok

-3 hours

The most useful stopover on the Australia to Europe run. Hot and humid year round with afternoon storms from November to January. Hawker centres keep food costs low even though accommodation is not cheap. Around $210 a day.

The default family trip for a reason: short flight, no jet lag, resorts set up for children. May to October is the dry season. Cyclone season runs November to April, which is exactly why travel insurance matters here. Around $280 a day for a family of four sharing.

A long way to go for ten days. Australians usually pair it with a stopover in Asia or the Middle East. Aim for May, or September and October: July and August are hot, crowded and the most expensive. Around $330 a day mid-range, excluding flights.

How we write these guides

So you know what the numbers mean before you plan against them.

Budgets

Daily budget figures are per person, per day, in Australian dollars, and cover accommodation on a twin-share basis, food, local transport and one paid activity. They exclude international flights, travel insurance and shopping. Mid-range means a three-star hotel or a well-reviewed guesthouse, restaurant meals rather than fine dining, and public transport with the occasional taxi.

Seasons

Best-season advice is written for Australian travellers. Where our school holidays or public holidays collide with a destination's peak, we say so, because that is when both flights and accommodation are dearest.

Visas and entry

Visa notes describe the general position for Australian passport holders travelling as tourists. They are a starting point, not a determination. Entry rules change without notice and can differ by purpose of travel, length of stay and passport validity. Always check the destination government's official source and the Smartraveller advisory before you book.

Safety notes

Safety sections summarise commonly reported risks. They are not a substitute for the current Smartraveller advisory level, which can change quickly. See our Travel Advice Disclaimer.
