Itinerary 03 · Japan · 10 days

Ten days in Japan

Tokyo, Hakone, Kyoto, Nara and Osaka using three bases instead of six, structured around an overnight flight from the Australian east coast.

01

Overview

Fly into Tokyo and out of Osaka Kansai. Almost every carrier serving Australia prices this open-jaw the same as a return into Tokyo, and it saves a wasted three-hour backtrack at the end.

The structure is deliberately conservative: four nights Tokyo, four nights Kyoto, two nights Osaka. Two day trips, to Hakone and Nara, use the base rather than a new hotel. You unpack three times in ten days instead of six.

On rail passes. This route involves three long-distance legs: Tokyo to Kyoto, a Kyoto to Nara return, and Kyoto to Osaka. Bought individually those cost less than a seven-day Japan Rail Pass at current prices, so this itinerary assumes individual tickets. If you add Hiroshima or a return to Tokyo, run the numbers again, because the answer flips.

Who this suits

  • Two adults sharing a room, which is what all costs assume
  • Anyone with two weeks off, or eight days of leave bridged by two weekends
  • First-time visitors to Japan. Everything here is well signposted in English
  • Reasonable walkers. Several days involve 12 to 15 kilometres on foot

What to change with more time

With fourteen days, add two nights in Kanazawa or Takayama between Tokyo and Kyoto, and one night in a ryokan at Hakone rather than day-tripping it.

02

Day 1

Day 01

Arrive Tokyo, Asakusa and Ueno

Estimated day cost$196

  • 06:30 Land at Haneda or Narita Buy a rechargeable IC card at the station machine before anything else, and load about $50. It works on trains, subways, buses and in convenience stores nationwide. Train 45 min to Asakusa
  • 09:00 Drop bags at the hotel Every Japanese hotel will hold luggage before check-in without fuss or charge. Walk 10 min
  • 10:00 Senso-ji and Nakamise-dori Tokyo's oldest temple, founded in 645. The approach street is a wall of people from about eleven, so ten is the last quiet hour. Ninety minutes. Walk 20 min across the Sumida River
  • 12:30 Lunch: soba or tempura in Asakusa Around $15 a head. Vending machine ordering at the door is normal and staff will help if you hesitate. Train 20 min to Ueno
  • 14:30 Ueno Park and the Tokyo National Museum A deliberately low-effort afternoon for a day when you left Sydney last night. Modest entry, and the best collection in the country. Train 15 min back to Asakusa
  • 18:00 Early izakaya dinner, then sleep Eat at six, sleep at nine. Pushing through to midnight is how people lose day two.

Includes the airport train, first IC card load, two meals and mid-range Tokyo accommodation. Confined to one part of the city on purpose.

03

Day 2

Day 02

Meiji Jingu, Harajuku, Shibuya, Shinjuku

Estimated day cost$228

  • 08:00 Convenience store breakfast Onigiri, coffee and fruit for about $6. This is a normal Japanese breakfast, not a compromise. Train 25 min to Meiji-jingumae
  • 09:00 Meiji Jingu A forested approach in the middle of the city, quietest before ten. Bow at the torii, and follow the hand-washing sequence at the temizuya. One hour. Walk 10 min
  • 10:30 Takeshita-dori and Omotesando Two streets three minutes apart doing completely different things: teenage retail chaos, then international flagship architecture. Walk 25 min south
  • 12:30 Shibuya Crossing and lunch Book a timed slot for the Shibuya Sky observation deck in advance if you want the view from above. Around $28 a head for a good lunch nearby. Train 10 min to Shinjuku
  • 15:30 Shinjuku Gyoen A large formal garden with a small entry fee, and the calmest place within a kilometre of Shinjuku Station. Ninety minutes. Walk 20 min
  • 18:30 Dinner in Omoide Yokocho Tiny grills under the railway. Some places have a cover charge, posted on a sign at the door. Read it before you sit.

Around 14 kilometres on foot. Wear shoes you have already broken in, and note that Tokyo has almost no public bins, so you will carry rubbish until you find a convenience store.

04

Day 3

Day 03

Day trip to Hakone

Estimated day cost$254

  • 07:30 Romancecar from Shinjuku Reserved seats. Buy a Hakone Free Pass, which covers the train plus the whole loop of mountain railway, cable car, ropeway and boat, and is much better value than paying separately. Train 1 h 25 min · 85 km to Hakone-Yumoto
  • 09:15 Hakone Tozan mountain railway to Gora A switchback railway climbing through the hills, reversing direction three times. Forty minutes and an attraction in its own right. Cable car then ropeway, 35 min
  • 10:45 Owakudani volcanic valley Sulphur vents and, on a clear day, Mount Fuji. The ropeway closes at short notice when gas levels rise, so keep a fallback in mind: the Hakone Open-Air Museum at Gora. Ropeway 25 min down to Togendai
  • 12:30 Lake Ashi crossing Included in the pass. Touristy, and the best angle on Fuji if the weather cooperates. Boat 30 min to Hakone-machi
  • 14:00 Hakone Shrine and the lakeside torii The queue for the torii photograph runs to 40 minutes. The cedar avenue behind the shrine is empty and better. Bus 45 min back to Hakone-Yumoto
  • 16:00 Day-use onsen Several bathhouses sit near the station. Wash thoroughly before entering, no swimwear, no towel in the water. Tattoos are refused at some places, so check before paying. Train 1 h 25 min back to Shinjuku

The most expensive Tokyo-based day because of the pass and the onsen. It is also the day most people remember. Around 3 hours 20 minutes of travel in total.

05

Day 4

Day 04

Tsukiji, then the Shinkansen to Kyoto

Estimated day cost$318

  • 07:30 Tsukiji Outer Market The wholesale auction moved to Toyosu, but the outer market still works and is best before nine. Eat standing up: tamagoyaki, grilled scallops, uni. Train 25 min back to the hotel
  • 10:00 Check out and send the big bag ahead Takkyubin luggage forwarding to your Kyoto hotel costs around $20 a bag and arrives next day. Travel with a day pack. Front desks arrange it. Train 25 min to Tokyo Station
  • 11:30 Shinkansen to Kyoto Reserve seats. Buy an ekiben boxed lunch on the platform, which is a genuine part of the experience. Sit on the right-hand side leaving Tokyo for the Fuji view about 40 minutes in. Train 2 h 20 min · 450 km to Kyoto
  • 14:30 Check in, Kyoto Four nights here. Near Kyoto Station is practical; Gion is atmospheric and dearer. Train 6 min to Inari
  • 16:30 Fushimi Inari Taisha Thousands of vermilion torii climbing the mountain. It is open 24 hours and free, so late afternoon empties out as the day crowds leave. Walking to the Yotsutsuji intersection takes about 45 minutes uphill and is where most people turn back. Train 6 min back into town
  • 19:00 Dinner near Kyoto Station or Pontocho Pontocho is a narrow lane of small restaurants along the Kamo River. Prices vary enormously, so read the menu outside.

The most expensive day of the trip because of the Shinkansen fare and luggage forwarding. Both are worth it.

06

Day 5

Day 05

Higashiyama: Kiyomizu-dera to Gion on foot

Estimated day cost$232

  • 07:30 Kiyomizu-dera at opening Opens at six in most seasons. Arriving before eight is the difference between a temple and a queue. The main hall stands on a 13 metre timber stage built without nails. Walk 10 min downhill
  • 09:00 Sannenzaka and Ninenzaka Two preserved stone lanes of timber machiya. Photography of the streets is fine; photographing geiko and maiko in the private lanes of Gion is restricted and fined. Walk 15 min north
  • 10:30 Kodai-ji and Yasaka Shrine Kodai-ji has a bamboo grove a fraction the size of Arashiyama's and a fraction of the people. Walk 10 min
  • 12:00 Lunch in Gion A set lunch at a place that costs a fortune at dinner is the standard trick, around $30 a head. Walk 20 min north along the river
  • 14:00 Nanzen-ji and the Philosopher's Path Nanzen-ji has a brick aqueduct running through the grounds. The Philosopher's Path is a 2 kilometre canal-side walk north to Ginkaku-ji, about 40 minutes. Walk 40 min
  • 16:00 Ginkaku-ji, the Silver Pavilion Never actually silvered. The dry sand garden is the reason to go. Closes at 17:00, so do not leave it later. Bus 30 min back into town

About 13 kilometres on foot with three paid temple entries. Kyoto is a bus city rather than a train city, and the buses get very full between 16:00 and 18:00.

07

Day 6

Day 06

Arashiyama and the north-west

Estimated day cost$238

  • 07:30 Arashiyama bamboo grove The grove is a five-minute walk and it is genuinely crowded from nine. At half seven it is close to empty, which is the entire trick. Walk 10 min
  • 08:30 Tenryu-ji temple garden A 14th-century garden designed to borrow the mountains behind it. Opens at 08:30. Allow an hour. Walk 20 min across the Togetsukyo Bridge
  • 10:30 Iwatayama Monkey Park A steep 20-minute climb to a hilltop troop of Japanese macaques and the best view over Kyoto. Do not make eye contact with the monkeys and do not carry food in your hands. Train 25 min east
  • 13:00 Lunch near Ryoan-ji Simple noodle places around the temple, about $16 a head. Walk 5 min
  • 14:00 Ryoan-ji rock garden Fifteen stones in raked gravel, arranged so that one is always hidden from any viewing position. Forty minutes, and better if you sit still for twenty of them. Bus 15 min · 3 km
  • 15:30 Kinkaku-ji, the Golden Pavilion One fixed circuit, no interior access, about 45 minutes. Busy at any hour, and worth it once. Bus 40 min back into town
  • 18:30 Nishiki Market and dinner A covered market five blocks long. Many stalls close by 18:00, so this is more about dinner in the surrounding streets.

A bus-heavy day. A one-day Kyoto bus pass pays for itself after three trips. Three paid temple entries included.

08

Day 7

Day 07

Day trip to Nara

Estimated day cost$224

  • 08:30 Train from Kyoto to Nara Either the JR line or the Kintetsu line. Kintetsu drops you closer to the park. Train 45 min · 40 km
  • 09:30 Nara Park and the deer More than a thousand sika deer roam the park. Deer crackers are sold at stalls. They are wild animals, they bite and headbutt, and they will follow food in a bag. Do not tease them with a cracker you are not going to hand over. Walk 15 min
  • 10:30 Todai-ji and the Great Buddha One of the largest timber buildings in the world, holding a 15 metre bronze Buddha cast in 752. Ninety minutes. Walk 20 min uphill
  • 12:30 Lunch in Naramachi The old merchant quarter, south of the park. Around $22 a head and far quieter than the park cafes. Walk 20 min back north-east
  • 14:00 Kasuga Taisha Three thousand stone and bronze lanterns along the approach and through the shrine. The forest path in is the best part. Walk 30 min back to the station
  • 16:30 Train back to Kyoto Back with an evening free, which is the point of day-tripping rather than moving hotels.

The cheapest full day of the trip. Todai-ji entry is the only significant admission. Around 8 kilometres on foot.

09

Day 8

Day 08

Kyoto to Osaka: castle and Dotonbori

Estimated day cost$246

  • 09:30 Check out and take the train to Osaka A short, cheap leg on a local express. No need for the Shinkansen. Train 30 min · 43 km
  • 10:30 Drop bags, then Osaka Castle The keep is a 1931 reconstruction with a museum inside and a view from the top. The grounds and moat are the better part, and they are free. Train 20 min · 6 km
  • 13:00 Lunch: okonomiyaki Osaka's dish, cooked on a hotplate at the table. Around $18 a head. Walk 15 min
  • 15:00 Shinsekai and Tsutenkaku A pre-war entertainment district that has aged into something odd and likeable. Kushikatsu is the local food, and the rule about not double-dipping the shared sauce is enforced with real feeling. Train 10 min north
  • 17:30 Check in, Namba Two nights. Namba is the best-connected base for the airport train on day ten. Walk 10 min
  • 19:00 Dotonbori after dark Neon over the canal, and the densest eating street in the country. Takoyaki, kushikatsu, ramen. Budget $40 and graze rather than sitting down once.

Includes the Kyoto to Osaka fare, castle entry and a food-heavy evening. Very little walking distance compared with the Kyoto days.

10

Day 9

Day 09

Himeji Castle, or a slower day in Osaka

Estimated day cost$264

  • 08:00 Shinkansen to Himeji The option worth taking if you have the energy. Himeji is the finest surviving original castle in Japan and never fell to a siege. Train 30 min · 90 km from Shin-Osaka
  • 09:00 Himeji Castle A 15-minute walk straight up the avenue from the station. Allow two hours: the keep involves steep timber stairs and you remove your shoes to climb it. Walk 5 min
  • 11:30 Koko-en garden Nine walled Edo-period gardens beside the castle, on a combined ticket. Forty-five minutes and almost empty. Train 30 min back to Osaka
  • 14:00 Late lunch in Umeda The department store food halls under Umeda Station are a genuine attraction. Around $20 a head. Lift to the 39th floor
  • 16:00 Umeda Sky Building floating garden An open-air rooftop observatory connected by an escalator through the gap between two towers. Best an hour before sunset. Train 15 min south
  • 18:30 Last dinner, and pack If you skipped Himeji, use the day for Kaiyukan aquarium or a half day of shopping in Shinsaibashi instead.

The Himeji return fare and combined castle ticket make this the second most expensive day. Dropping Himeji brings it to about $180.

11

Day 10

Day 10

Departure from Kansai

Estimated day cost$180

  • 08:00 Breakfast and last-minute buying Convenience stores and the Namba department stores open early. Tax-free purchases need your passport at the counter, and some items must stay sealed until you leave the country. Walk 10 min to Namba Station
  • 10:30 Nankai Rapi:t or airport express to Kansai Reserved seat, about 40 minutes from Namba. Buy the ticket the night before if you are travelling in a peak period. Train 40 min · 45 km
  • 11:30 Kansai International Airport Arrive three hours before an international departure. Spend leftover IC card balance at the convenience store rather than trying to refund it.
  • 14:00 Depart for Australia Most Australia-bound services leave Kansai in the late morning or early afternoon and land the same evening.

Includes the final night of accommodation carried into the day rate and the airport transfer. Nothing scheduled, on purpose.

Ways to spend less

  • Drop the Himeji day trip and stay in Osaka. That is about $85 saved.
  • Eat one convenience store or standing-noodle meal a day. Japanese convenience store food is genuinely good and costs a third of a restaurant.
  • Choose business hotels over boutique. Rooms are small, spotless and half the price.
  • Travel in early June or late November rather than during blossom or autumn colour peaks.

Do not buy a rail pass for this route

Three legs of Tokyo to Kyoto, Kyoto to Nara return and Kyoto to Osaka, plus a Himeji return, come to less than a seven-day nationwide pass at current prices. If you add Hiroshima or return to Tokyo at the end, recalculate, because the answer changes.

Before you go

  • Passport and entry. Australian passport holders currently receive visa-free short-stay entry for tourism. Confirm the current rule and complete any required online arrival registration before you fly.
  • Medication. Japan restricts some medicines that are ordinary in Australia, including certain cold and flu preparations and stimulant prescriptions. Check the rules, carry original packaging and a doctor's letter.
  • Power. 100 V, plug type A, flat two-pin. Australian plugs will not fit. Bring an adaptor and check anything with a heating element.
  • Cash. Still needed. Use post office and convenience store ATMs, which accept international cards; many bank ATMs do not.
  • Connectivity. An eSIM or pocket wifi is far cheaper than Australian roaming. See our eSIM and roaming guide.
  • Luggage. Use takkyubin forwarding between cities. Large suitcases on the Tokaido Shinkansen require an oversized baggage reservation, free when booked with the seat.
  • Etiquette. No tipping. Phones on silent on trains. Shoes off where indicated. Carry your rubbish.
  • Insurance. Medical care is excellent and expensive for visitors, and typhoons can cancel trains and flights at short notice. Read the policy's product disclosure statement.
  • Emergency numbers in Japan: 110 for police, 119 for fire and ambulance.
